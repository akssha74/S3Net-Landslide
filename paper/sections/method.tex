\section{Controlled methods}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Band semantics and spectral controls}

Let $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times4\times H\times W}$ denote the released HR-GLDD arrays. Green and NIR occupy columns 1 and 3 under both plausible orders; Red is column 0 under RGBN and column 2 under native-product BGRN. The paper names the selected bands but not their NumPy positions, while the official notebook directly displays columns 0--2 as RGB without exposing array construction. We therefore run the full matrix for each candidate order $o\in\{\mathrm{RGBN},\mathrm{BGRN}\}$ and define the candidate normalized-difference index
\begin{equation}
V_o=\frac{X_{\mathrm{NIR}}-X_{\mathrm{Red}(o)}}
        {X_{\mathrm{NIR}}+X_{\mathrm{Red}(o)}+10^{-6}}.
\end{equation}
We compare three two-channel controls:
\begin{align}
C_{\mathrm{zero}} &= (0,0),\\
C_{\mathrm{raw},o} &= (|\nabla X_{\mathrm{Red}(o)}|,|\nabla X_{\mathrm{NIR}}|),\\
C_{\mathrm{index},o} &= (V_o,|\nabla V_o|).
\end{align}
The zero control preserves attention parameters while removing external spectral information. The raw control tests whether generic optical edges explain any candidate-index advantage. Every recorded HR-GLDD output declares its candidate order; neither candidate is declared authoritative.

\subsection{Semantic-identifiability certificate}

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the finite set of metadata-consistent schemas and
$\delta(o)$ a signed contrast under schema $o$. We define its semantic envelope
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_{\delta}=
\left[\min_{o\in\mathcal{O}}\delta(o),\,
      \max_{o\in\mathcal{O}}\delta(o)\right].
\end{equation}
The effect direction is semantically identified only when
$0\notin\mathcal{E}_{\delta}$; otherwise it is direction-unidentified. Here
$\mathcal{O}=\{\mathrm{RGBN},\mathrm{BGRN}\}$. This is a finite schema
sensitivity certificate over descriptive means, not a confidence interval or
a claim that no unlisted schema exists.

\subsection{Capacity-matched residual attention}

All controlled-attention models use the same residual encoder--decoder with channels $(32,64,128,256)$ and three skip gates. At scale $l$, the two control maps are bilinearly resized and concatenated with encoder features $F_l$:
\begin{equation}
M_l=\sigma\!\left[\operatorname{Conv}_{1\times1}
\left(\operatorname{ReLU}\left(\operatorname{BN}
\left(\operatorname{Conv}_{3\times3}[F_l,C_l]\right)\right)\right)\right],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
F_{l,\mathrm{out}}=F_l\odot M_l+F_l.
\end{equation}
Every control mode therefore has 2,114,084 trainable parameters. U-Net and ResU-Net are included as architectural baselines.

\subsection{Loss controls}

For pixel $i$, the unreduced base-loss map combines binary cross-entropy and focal loss \citep{lin2017focal}, with focal positive-class weight $\alpha=0.35$ and focusing exponent 2:
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\mathrm{base},i}=\tfrac12\ell_{\mathrm{BCE},i}+\tfrac12\ell_{\mathrm{focal},i}.
\end{equation}
Let $B_{\mathrm{GT}}=\operatorname{Dilate}_{3\times3}(Y)-\operatorname{Erode}_{3\times3}(Y)$ denote the two-sided, one-pixel-radius Chebyshev morphological boundary band. We compare:
\begin{align}
L_{\mathrm{plain}} &=
  \operatorname{mean}_i\{\ell_{\mathrm{base},i}[1+\gamma B_{\mathrm{GT},i}]\},\\
L_{\mathrm{index},o} &=
  \operatorname{mean}_i\{\ell_{\mathrm{base},i}[1+\gamma \widetilde{B_{\mathrm{GT}}|\nabla V_o|}_i]\},
\end{align}
where $\gamma=2$. For an informative index-boundary signal, the tilde rescales it within each image to the mean mass of $B_{\mathrm{GT}}$; when its mean is at most $10^{-6}$, it falls back to $B_{\mathrm{GT}}$. Under both candidate orders, 8 of 1,119 training images use this fallback and the maximum mass mismatch is $2.98\times10^{-8}$. Thus $L_{\mathrm{plain}}$ versus $L_{\mathrm{index},o}$ changes spatial allocation without changing average added weight.

\subsection{Evaluation quantities}

We compute pooled F1, intersection over union, precision, recall, background false-positive rate (FPR), and per-tile macro F1; the compact main table reports the quantities used by the claims. Boundary F1 is computed per tile after extracting predicted and reference boundary bands; a boundary pixel matches when the opposite boundary lies within a one-pixel Chebyshev (8-neighbour) tolerance.

For an operating-point comparison, U-Net recall at threshold $0.5$ is measured on validation data. Every arm, including U-Net, is then mapped to the exact validation-score breakpoint nearest that recall, with ties assigned to the higher threshold, and the selected threshold is applied unchanged to test probabilities. Test labels do not select thresholds.

To isolate spatial degradation without changing sensor or region, each PlanetScope test patch is antialiased from $128\times128$ to $38\times38$ (approximately the 3~m-to-10~m ratio) and restored to $128\times128$ before inference. This experiment measures controlled information loss; it is not a simulation of Sentinel-2 radiometry.
