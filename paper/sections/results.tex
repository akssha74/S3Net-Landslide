\textit{1) Comparative Performance:} Vanilla U-Net achieves $68.33\% \pm 0.27\%$ micro F1 and $54.84\% \pm 0.65\%$ macro F1. ResU-Net achieves $69.66\% \pm 0.89\%$ micro F1 and $57.92\% \pm 1.32\%$ macro F1. Proposed S$^3$-Net attains the highest overall performance among lightweight models, reaching $\mathbf{70.18\% \pm 0.25\%}$ micro F1, $\mathbf{54.06\% \pm 0.29\%}$ IoU, and $\mathbf{58.52\% \pm 0.24\%}$ macro F1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}). S$^3$-Net achieves the highest recall ($\mathbf{66.59\% \pm 1.95\%}$) while demonstrating lower seed variance ($\sigma = 0.25\%$ vs. $0.89\%$).

\textit{2) Multi-Index Ablation and Operating Trade-off:} Comparing alternative gating formulations across 3 seeds shows that normalized spectral indices (NDVI $69.95\% \pm 0.30\%$ micro F1, $65.73\%$ recall; NDWI $69.96\% \pm 0.10\%$ micro F1, $65.51\%$ recall) and raw spatial gradients without band ratios ($69.75\% \pm 0.22\%$ micro F1, $64.94\%$ recall) all outperform unguided attention ($69.26\% \pm 0.44\%$, $62.81\%$ recall), confirming that spatial edge modulation—whether derived from vegetation contrast (NDVI) or exposed wet subsoil (NDWI)—recovers narrow scarp boundaries. Across the $52.35\text{ km}^2$ test background, S$^3$-Net operates with a background false-positive rate (FPR) of $2.84\% \pm 0.36\%$ ($1.32\text{ km}^2$ FP area), compared to $2.41\% \pm 0.12\%$ ($1.12\text{ km}^2$) for U-Net, $2.56\% \pm 0.36\%$ ($1.19\text{ km}^2$) for ResU-Net, and $2.28\% \pm 0.09\%$ ($1.06\text{ km}^2$) for the ablation. S$^3$-Net's precision ($74.31\%$) represents an operational trade-off recovering $+4.51\%$ recall over U-Net. On 144 low-prevalence confounding tiles ($<5\%$ landslide area; $98.2\%$ background), S$^3$-Net maintains an operational background FPR of $1.37\%$ ($0.28\text{ km}^2$ FP area) with $47.33\%$ precision.

\textit{3) Spectral Cluster Disaggregation:} Evaluating across 10 unsupervised spectral clusters (KMeans on reflectance and NDVI means) shows S$^3$-Net outperforms U-Net across 9 of 10 clusters ($+7.25\%$ in Cluster 4, $+4.82\%$ in Cluster 2, $+3.47\%$ in Cluster 7). In Cluster 5 (mean NDVI $0.17$, 4 tiles), all optical models drop (F1 $<1\%$), empirically reflecting conditions where sparse pre-existing vegetation limits optical scarp contrast.

\textit{4) Topographic Illumination Invariance:} Evaluating on shadowed mountain tiles ($n=89$, mean radiance $<0.20$), S$^3$-Net achieves a $+2.99\%$ F1 gain over vanilla U-Net ($60.87\% \pm 1.62\%$ vs. $57.88\% \pm 0.33\%$) and $+1.49\%$ over the unguided ablation ($60.87\%$ vs. $59.38\%$), nearly double the gain on sunlit slopes ($+1.67\%$ and $+0.77\%$), empirically validating the theoretical illumination cancellation mechanism.

\textit{5) Edge Deployment Profiling vs. Heavy Models:} DCA-UNet (Song et al., 2026~\cite{dcaunet2026}) achieves higher raw accuracy ($74.41\%$ F1, $59.24\%$ IoU) but requires 29.50M parameters. S$^3$-Net achieves $70.18\%$ micro F1 with only 2.11M parameters ($\approx 14\times$ fewer parameters, 2.16~ms/tile latency on PyTorch-MPS, 462.6~tiles/s throughput). Profiling via ONNX Runtime demonstrates a standalone model size of $8.20\text{ MB}$ (ONNX Opset 18) with $8.19\text{ ms/tile}$ CPU latency and $2.10\text{ ms/tile}$ ONNX-MPS latency ($476.4\text{ tiles/s}$), confirming edge payload deployability. Serialized ONNX weights and all 12 three-seed prediction arrays are publicly released under Release v1.0.0.

\subsection{Statistical Significance via Cluster Bootstrap}
Because satellite patches within regional disaster zones exhibit spatial grouping, standard $i.i.d.$ hypothesis tests risk underestimating parameter variance. We perform paired 1,000-draw cluster-bootstrap resampling treating each of the 355 test image tiles as an independent cluster, where each tile's metric is averaged across all three random seeds:
(i)~\textit{S$^3$-Net vs. Vanilla U-Net:} S$^3$-Net achieves a mean paired macro F1 gain of $+3.69\%$ ($+0.0369$; $+1.85\%$ micro F1), with a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of $[+2.90\%, +4.51\%]$ and $p < 0.001$, strictly excluding zero and confirming Hypothesis~H1.
(ii)~\textit{S$^3$-Net vs. ResU-Net Baseline:} S$^3$-Net achieves a statistically confirmed macro F1 gain of $+0.60\%$ ($+0.0060$; $+0.52\%$ micro F1), with a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of $[+0.17\%, +1.01\%]$ and $p = 0.005$. On micro F1, the architectures are closely matched ($70.18\%$ vs. $69.66\%$), with S$^3$-Net exhibiting substantially lower seed variance ($\sigma = 0.25\%$ vs. $0.89\%$), confirming improved training stability.
(iii)~\textit{S$^3$-Net vs. Unguided Attention Ablation:} Reintroducing physical spectral scarp gating yields a mean paired macro F1 gain of $+1.44\%$ ($+0.0144$), with a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of $[+1.08\%, +1.83\%]$ and $p < 0.001$. The statistical significance confirms that the performance improvement originates specifically from physical vegetation scarp contrast rather than added convolutional parameters.

\subsection{Cross-Regime Generalization and Resolution Boundary}
To assess out-of-distribution generalizability across distinct environmental regimes, we conduct a 10-fold Leave-One-Spectral-Cluster-Out (LOSCO) cross-validation benchmark across all 1,758 patches. In each fold $k \in \{0, \dots, 9\}$, models are trained on the remaining 9 spectral clusters and evaluated zero-shot on the held-out 10th cluster without fine-tuning.

Under zero-shot transfer, S$^3$-Net achieves a cross-cluster macro F1 of $\mathbf{53.11\% \pm 17.76\%}$ (micro F1: $\mathbf{60.93\% \pm 20.12\%}$), outperforming vanilla U-Net ($49.92\% \pm 19.21\%$ macro F1, $58.00\% \pm 21.53\%$ micro F1) by $+3.19\%$ macro F1 ($+2.93\%$ micro F1) and outperforming ResU-Net ($51.39\% \pm 18.58\%$ macro F1, $59.80\% \pm 20.71\%$ micro F1) by $+1.72\%$ macro F1 ($+1.13\%$ micro F1). S$^3$-Net maintains the highest zero-shot accuracy across major unseen regimes (e.g., $61.33\%$ micro F1 on Cluster 7 with 351 patches, $+5.72\%$ over U-Net; $52.16\%$ micro F1 on Cluster 4, $+4.87\%$ over U-Net). Conversely, evaluating zero-shot on 245 Sentinel-2 patches ($10\text{ m}$ GSD) from Landslide4Sense~\cite{ghorbanzadeh2022landslide4sense} drops all models to F1 $< 15\%$ (U-Net $14.26\%$, ablation $11.31\%$, ResU-Net $10.00\%$, S$^3$-Net $9.91\%$), showing that scarp attention kernels trained at 3~m confer no advantage under a $3.3\times$ spatial resolution mismatch ($384\text{ m}$ vs. $1,280\text{ m}$ footprint) and establishing an empirical sensor-resolution coupling boundary.
